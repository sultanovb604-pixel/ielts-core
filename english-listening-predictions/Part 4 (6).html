SECTION 4

Today, we'll continue that discussion by looking at dormancy. First, we'll cover what dormancy is and why organisms become dormant. Dormancy is a state of inactivity that organisms or living things enter when they need to conserve the resources available to them and reduce their demands on their environment. [Q31 → resources]

Most major groups of plants and animals have some representatives that can become dormant. What is the value of dormancy? To start with, animals can become dormant and survive conditions that are stressful in environmental terms. For instance, when lakes, ponds, or rivers dry up, aquatic organisms that can enter dormancy can survive the drought, while others who cannot do this will die. [Q32 → stressful]

Another advantage is that creatures that can become dormant in inhospitable conditions, such as extreme cold, can live in a larger range of places than other organisms. Because they can live where other animals cannot survive when they are active and looking for food, the amount of competition they have to deal with from other regions is greatly reduced. [Q33 → competition]

Let's move on to look at what causes dormancy. There are a number of variables, but the ones that are the most significant are temperature changes and the amount of light organisms get. Availability of food, water, and oxygen is also very important. [Q34 → light]

Now, we'll explore some specific examples. Let us first look at dormancy in insects. Insects experience a reduced metabolism, a form of dormancy called diapause in insects. Diapause is either a reaction to difficult environmental conditions, or an unnecessary part of the life cycle, such as with butterflies or certain moths. [Q35 → reduced]

In terms of environment, it's interesting to know that through dormancy, insects can survive when it's very cold. Another creature we'll look at is fish.
