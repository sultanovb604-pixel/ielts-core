Task 2 Band 8.5–9.0 Model Causes & Solutions

Depletion of Natural Resources

IELTS Essay Prompt

Natural resources such as oil, forests, and fresh water are being consumed at an alarming rate. What problems does this cause? How can we solve these problems?

Introduction

The rapid depletion of vital natural resources such as fossil fuels, timber, and potable water has become a critical global concern with far-reaching ecological and socio-economic ramifications. If left unaddressed, this overconsumption threatens planetary stability, food security, and human health. Addressing these challenges requires a concerted tripartite approach combining robust regulation, technological innovation, and structural behavioral changes.

Body Paragraph 1 — Problems Precipitated by Resource Depletion

The unbridled exploitation of natural capital precipitates two immediate and catastrophic crises. Environmentally, the destruction of primary forests and the combustion of fossil fuels accelerate climate disruption, degrade biodiversity, and cause widespread habitat fragmentation. Furthermore, the exhaustion of subterranean aquifers and freshwater reserves directly undermines agricultural yields, triggering acute food shortages and potential geopolitical conflicts over water rights. Socio-economically, relying on depleting non-renewable commodities leads to extreme price volatility, which disproportionately harms developing nations and exacerbates global economic inequality.

Body Paragraph 2 — Strategic and Sustainable Solutions

To mitigate these hazards, international bodies and sovereign governments must enact comprehensive policy frameworks. First, state authorities should impose stringent caps on carbon emissions and industrial water usage while eliminating subsidies for fossil fuel extraction. Concurrently, public capital should be redirected toward renewable energy infrastructure — such as solar, wind, and geothermal grids — and advanced desalination technology to replenish potable water supplies. On a societal level, promoting a circular economic model that incentivizes recycling, waste minimization, and energy-efficient product design will reduce baseline aggregate demand for virgin materials.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the unsustainable consumption of natural resources endangers environmental equilibrium and economic stability worldwide. Nevertheless, through decisive regulatory interventions, heavy investment in clean technologies, and a transition to circular production models, the international community can ensure the sustainable preservation of finite resources for future generations.

Key Band 9.0 Lexical Resource

unbridled exploitation of natural capital Unrestrained, excessive consumption of planetary environmental assets.
far-reaching ecological ramifications Extensive, wide-ranging consequences for natural ecosystems.
exhaustion of subterranean aquifers Depleting underground reservoirs containing fresh groundwater.
circular economic model A production and consumption cycle that recycles materials endlessly.
aggregate demand for virgin materials The total overall market requirement for freshly extracted natural raw inputs.
tripartite approach A comprehensive strategy involving three distinct, coordinated pillars.
