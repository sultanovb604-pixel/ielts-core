PART 4

Today I'd like to look at another type of amphibian, the African clawed frog, whose Latin name is Xenopus laevis. Its name comes from the three short claws on each hind foot, which it uses to tear apart its food. The Latin word xenopus means "strange foot" and laevis means "smooth".
I'll start with a general description. The African clawed frog can be commonly found in the eastern and southern parts of Africa. They have a flattened head and body but no tongue or external ears. As the Latin name suggests, African clawed frogs have smooth skin, unlike the toad, whose skin has a rough texture. [Q31 → smooth]
The body is covered with mucus, which gives their sensitive skin protection, thus playing an indispensable role in their survival. [Q32 → protection]
Male and female frogs can be easily distinguished, and the major difference is size. Generally, male frogs are about half as big as females. Females are usually 4.5 inches long, while males are about 2.5 inches. [Q33 → half]

Now let's turn to their habitats. African clawed frogs are entirely aquatic, living in water all their life. Although they live in a wide range of freshwater environments, they particularly like still water instead of fast-flowing streams. [Q34 → still]
In the wild they're highly adaptable, whether in oases or glacier-covered lakes. They can even survive in water with a high level of salt, because they have a physiological mechanism that regulates the balance of water inside and outside the body. [Q35 → salt]
In dry weather, they burrow themselves into mud, where they can survive for more than a year. They're incredibly hardy and long-lived, with an average lifespan of 5 to 15 years. [Q36 → mud]

Now, the diet. They are carnivores that hunt insects, spiders, worms and fish. African clawed frogs depend on the sense of smell to detect their prey. [Q37 → smell]
They first use the claws on their hind feet to tear pieces of ample food. Then the front feet, which are not webbed, push food into the mouth, and meanwhile a distinctive pump system is used to draw or suck food into the mouth. [Q38 → pump]

So they're voracious predators that adapt quickly to many habitats. In some parts of the world they act like an invasive species. As a result, they're illegal to own, transport or sell without a permit in some states of America, such as California, Washington and Arizona, where they've been seen as a type of pest. [Q39 → pest]
But not many people know that African clawed frogs are also used in medicine. As mentioned, their skin is covered with mucus, which has antibiotic properties. This can help kill bacteria and cure infection. So we shouldn't ignore the medical value of the African clawed frog. [Q40 → antibiotic]
